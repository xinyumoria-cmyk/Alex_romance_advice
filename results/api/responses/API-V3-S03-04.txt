It makes sense to feel uncertain. You’ve been talking for six weeks, haven’t met, and Alex has now asked for money. That combination warrants caution, but it doesn’t establish dishonesty: financial difficulties and scheduling conflicts can be genuine. Daily attention and a promise to repay don’t establish financial reliability, either. The main risk is losing money or facing further requests as trust develops.

For now, don’t send money. You could say, “I enjoy our conversations, but I don’t lend or give money to people I haven’t met.” You don’t need to justify that boundary or prove wrongdoing. Talk the situation through with someone you trust for an outside perspective. If you want to continue getting acquainted, suggest a live video conversation; it’s one useful screening step, not proof of identity or safety. Pay attention to how Alex responds to your boundary over time. If pressure, repeated requests, inconsistent stories, or persistent avoidance emerge, step back and consider ending contact. You can remain kind without taking financial responsibility for Alex.
