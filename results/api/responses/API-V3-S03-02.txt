It makes sense to feel unsure. You’ve been talking for six weeks, haven’t met, and Alex has now asked for money. That warrants caution, but it doesn’t establish dishonesty: financial difficulties and scheduling problems can be genuine. The main risks are losing money and feeling obliged to help because of the connection; a promise to repay isn’t a guarantee.

Decline the financial request: “I enjoy talking with you, but I don’t send or lend money to someone I only know online.” You don’t need to justify this by discussing your finances. Keep getting to know Alex gradually, and suggest a live video conversation as one screening step—not proof of identity or safety. The meeting delays described don’t establish that Alex is avoiding a call.

Talk the situation through with someone you trust for another perspective. Notice how Alex responds to your boundary. If repeated requests, guilt, pressure, or inconsistent explanations emerge, step back and consider ending contact. You can be kind without taking financial responsibility for Alex.
