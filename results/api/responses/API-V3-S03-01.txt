It makes sense to feel unsure. You’ve talked for six weeks, haven’t met, and Alex has now asked for money. That warrants caution, but it doesn’t establish dishonesty: financial difficulties and scheduling problems can be genuine. Daily attention and a promise to repay don’t establish financial trust, either. The main risks are losing money and feeling obligated to provide more.

Decline the loan without needing to prove anything: “I enjoy talking with you, but I don’t send money to people I’ve only met online.” Keep your financial details private. Talk through the request with someone you trust for an outside perspective. If you want to continue getting acquainted, suggest a live video conversation and let trust develop gradually; a successful call would not prove safety or make lending advisable.

Notice how Alex responds to your boundary. If requests repeat, pressure develops, or explanations become inconsistent, step back and consider ending contact. You can be sympathetic to someone’s difficulty without taking responsibility for solving it financially.
