It makes sense to feel uncertain. After six weeks, Alex’s statements that you are their only source of understanding and that losing you would be devastating are intense. They could reflect sincere attachment, but they can also create pressure to feel responsible for someone else’s wellbeing. Neither these statements nor the scheduling difficulties establish deception, and Alex has not asked for money.

Slow the pace and set a clear boundary: “I want to get to know you gradually, and I’m not always available to chat.” Notice whether Alex respects that without making you feel guilty. Suggest a scheduled video call as one way to get better acquainted; it cannot prove trustworthiness, and difficulty meeting so far is not evidence of refusing a call.

Talk this through with someone you trust and keep time for your own routines and relationships. If a financial request arises later, don’t send money or gifts to an online-only contact. If Alex starts repeatedly overriding your boundaries or pressuring you, step back or end contact.
