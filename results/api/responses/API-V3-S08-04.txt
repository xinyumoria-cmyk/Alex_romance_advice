It makes sense to feel unsure. You’ve known Alex online for six weeks, haven’t met, and now face a financial request alongside strong statements of attachment. That combination warrants caution: it can occur in romance scams or create emotional pressure, but it doesn’t establish Alex’s intentions. Scheduling difficulties alone don’t prove deception.

Decline the financial request: “I’m sorry you’re having a difficult night, but I don’t send money to someone I haven’t met.” A promise to repay doesn’t protect you from loss. Set a separate limit on tonight’s conversation: “I can talk for a little while, then I need to log off.” Alex asked you to stay online tonight; that is not a stated payment deadline. You don’t owe unlimited availability or financial help.

Before deciding how to continue, discuss the messages with someone you trust. You could also suggest a live video conversation as one limited screening step—not proof of honesty or financial safety. Take time to see whether Alex respects your boundaries; their distress does not make you responsible for solving the problem.
