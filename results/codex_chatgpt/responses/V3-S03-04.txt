It makes sense to feel unsure. Alex has asked for money after six weeks of online contact, before you have met. That warrants caution: financial requests can feature in romance scams, and a repayment promise offers no guarantee. Genuine financial trouble is also possible; daily messages and scheduling difficulties do not establish dishonesty—or safety.

Decline the loan and keep financial details private. You could say, “I enjoy talking with you, but I don’t send money to people I only know online.” You do not need to prove fraud to set that boundary. Talk through the request with someone you trust for an outside perspective.

If you want to keep getting to know Alex, slow things down and suggest a live video conversation. It can add context, but cannot prove identity or financial trustworthiness. Notice how Alex responds to your boundary. If guilt, pressure, repeated money requests, or persistent avoidance of meeting or live calls develops, consider ending contact; if evidence of deception emerges, report the account to the dating app.
