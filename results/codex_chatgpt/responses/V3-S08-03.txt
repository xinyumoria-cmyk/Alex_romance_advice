Your uncertainty is understandable. Alex’s financial request, strong attachment after six weeks, and statements that only you understand them warrant caution together. They could reflect genuine distress, but they can also create pressure to give money. This does not establish fraud, and the meeting delays alone do not prove avoidance. A promise to repay offers no guarantee you would get your money back.

Don’t send money to someone you know only online. You can say, “I’m sorry things are difficult. I can’t help financially, and I need to log off tonight. We can talk another time.” You do not owe constant availability or financial help because Alex fears losing you. The request to stay online tonight is not itself a payment deadline.

Before continuing, discuss the messages with someone you trust. Ask Alex straightforward questions about what happened and suggest a live video conversation; a successful call would not prove safety. If Alex responds with repeated financial pressure, guilt, or contradictory explanations, step back and consider ending contact.
