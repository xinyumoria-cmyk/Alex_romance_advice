Your uncertainty is understandable. After six weeks without meeting, a money request alongside statements that you are the only person who understands Alex warrants caution. Their financial difficulty and attachment could be genuine, but this combination can also occur in romance scams or create emotional pressure. Scheduling difficulties alone do not prove dishonesty, and you are not responsible for Alex’s emotional wellbeing.

Decline the financial request: “I’m happy to keep getting to know you, but I don’t send money to someone I haven’t met.” A promise to repay does not protect you from losing money. Slow the pace and take time away from messaging if you need it; you don’t owe financial help to demonstrate care.

Discuss the messages with someone you trust for an outside perspective. Suggest a live video conversation as one limited screening step, not proof of safety. Notice how Alex responds to your boundary over time. If guilt, anger, repeated money requests or persistent avoidance of reasonable checks develop, consider ending contact and reporting concerning behaviour to the dating app.
